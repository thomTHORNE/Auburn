# Design Principles

These principles govern every feature — interaction, layout, defaults, and what is refused. They are not aesthetic preferences. They are the reason the app is shaped the way it is. If a proposed element or interaction cannot be justified by at least one of them, it does not belong in Auburn.

Each principle names the [Foundation](../Foundation.md) section it derives from. A principle that cannot trace to Foundation is either a missing part of Foundation or not a principle.

Each is stated as a rule, followed by the cost it prevents, followed by a line showing it in practice.

---

## The app stays out of the set

Between sets, Auburn asks for nothing beyond what the set is measured by. Every extra field, choice, or screen is attention taken from the body at the moment the body is what matters.

The cost is not the seconds. It is that a grade read by feel is only as good as the attention behind it, and attention spent on the phone is not spent on control and form.

> In practice: a set is a load and a grade. Reps are asked for only when the rolled modality counts them. During a workout, logging the next set is always the foremost action; everything else sits a level deeper.

*Derives from: The problem; A set is graded by feel.*

---

## Auburn shows, the user decides

Auburn presents what it knows — rest, history, notes — and leaves the judgment to the user. It never turns what it shows into an instruction.

Training is chosen by a body Auburn cannot feel. An app that decides on the user's behalf replaces the one source that knows with one that only counts, and the user stops learning to read their own body.

> In practice: the rest gauge shows days and a colour; it never says which muscle group to train. Weaknesses are named by the user, and notes are written by the user.

*Derives from: Rest and feel choose the training; Each exercise keeps its own notes; Weaknesses.*

---

## No escape hatches

The rules are the user's, and Auburn holds the user to them. Where a rule exists for accountability, Auburn offers no way around it.

A rule that bends whenever it is inconvenient stops being a rule. The value of being held to account is that it does not depend on how the user feels about it in the moment.

> In practice: a roll stands. A muscle group appears only once an exercise resets it. An overdue muscle group asks why, and the answer is kept.

*Derives from: Muscles set the pace; Rest and feel choose the training; Variety on purpose.*

---

## Structure earns its place

Nothing is added — a field, a grouping, a setting — unless it brings a direct benefit, and anything that stops bringing one goes.

Structure has upkeep. Unused structure is not neutral: it has to be filled in, ignored, or worked around, and it crowds out what is used. The Notion system that came before Auburn built fixed splits, intended reps, and an ignore-tracking flag, and abandoned each.

> In practice: a muscle group's split is optional. Fields that went unfilled in Notion were not carried into Auburn.

*Derives from: Training; The problem.*

---

## Built for the gym floor

Everything used during a workout can be hit with one thumb, without looking, by a hand that has just put down a weight.

The gym floor is a divided-attention context. A control that needs aiming pulls the eyes off the body and stretches the rest period around the phone; a mis-tap is a wrong set in the history.

> In practice: logging a set and grading it are within reach of the thumb of the hand holding the phone.

*Derives from: Training.*

---

## Every input answers at once

A tap registers the moment it lands, on screen and in the hand, before anything behind it completes.

"Did that register?" is answered by looking, which is what the gym floor cannot afford. An input with no immediate answer gets tapped twice, and a set logged twice or not at all corrupts the history that rest and progress are read from.

> In practice: a logged set is confirmed with a haptic pulse, so the phone can be put down without a glance.
> The boot screen is the one exception: it plays to the end, and a tap during it does nothing.

*Derives from: Training; A set is graded by feel.*
