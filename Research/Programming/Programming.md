# Programming

The method a goal is meant to be reached by, stated in advance by the user, so that the facts can show whether it was followed apart from whether it worked.

## Settled

- **Programming separates adherence from outcome.** A goal's gap shows whether its target is getting closer; programming shows whether the stated method was followed. Without it, "the method does not work" and "the method was not followed" look identical.
- **Programming is the user's intent, never Auburn's.** Auburn computes what the intent encodes and states it. It never suggests, derives, or triggers a change of method.
- **Only what the facts can measure is programming.** Intent the facts cannot measure stays in the goal's explanation.

## Open questions

- **Scope.** On a goal, on training as a whole, or both.
- **Changing the method.** Whether programming can be revised while the target holds, each revision dated and kept, or a new method makes a new goal.
- **What it encodes.** Frequency, sets per workout, and what else the facts can measure.
- **Where it shows.** Before, during, or after training, and on which screens.

## Findings

- **Targets set over years of training were almost never hit, and why was never knowable.** The gap could not tell a method that failed from a method not followed.
- **A target missed while the method was followed is as informative as one reached:** it says the method does not work.
- **The user's past programme was a frequency and a method, each held for about a year.** Three to four sessions a week, split as the gym and soreness allowed; rep counting for one cycle, grading for the next. It was a programme because it was stated in advance and kept.
- **A method can be planned to change mid-goal.** For a bodyweight pull-up out of reach: negatives and partial movements at a stated frequency, then more volume at a lighter load if heavier loading stops working.
- **Shown where training is chosen, programming would prescribe the day.** Foundation refuses that. Shown with a committed exercise, it is the user's own intent, as the exercise's notes are.

## Dependencies

- **Requires:** none.
- **Required by:** none.
